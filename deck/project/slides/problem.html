The problem

Can we predict a face from DNA — openly and honestly?

Reconstructing a 3D face from genotype is an open problem. The leading system, Difface, was trained on private data with an undisclosed SNP panel, and independent commentary argues its accuracy may reflect age, gender and ancestry more than genuine individual genetic signal.

No open, reproducible, demographically-controlled baseline exists on documented data.

Demographic and ancestry confounding is rarely separated from true genetic signal.

Visible appearance (eye, hair, skin colour) is also DNA-predictable, but needs the same honest evaluation.

The core problem: predicting a 3D face from DNA is genuinely hard, and the best-known system does it behind closed doors. Difface trained on private data, never released its SNP list, and critics showed its apparent accuracy might just be picking up age, gender and ancestry — not the individual's genetics. So there is no open, reproducible, confounder-controlled baseline anyone can verify. That gap is exactly what FaceCon fills — for both facial shape and visible appearance.
