    What the genetic data adds, and what it doesn't

    Every experiment compares against a baseline that knows only sex, age, height, weight and ancestry. A model only counts as using genetics if it beats that baseline and stops beating it when the genotypes are shuffled.

    Read these results with two limits in mind

    The faces are simulated, and the link from genotype to face is one we planted. It is additive, so a linear model is the correct model for it and is expected to win. These results show that our neural networks learn an additive signal less efficiently than ridge regression at this sample size. They cannot show how the networks would do if real facial genetics has non-additive structure.

    The planted genetic share of 20 per cent is a design choice, not an estimate. The real share could be much smaller.
